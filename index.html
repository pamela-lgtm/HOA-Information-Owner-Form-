Your HOA Information

What we need from you before your property goes to market, why it has to come from you, and what happens when it doesn't.

The short version

Your association's leasing rules govern your property whether or not anyone has read them. If we market and lease your property without knowing those rules, the association can block your resident's move-in, fine you, and pursue legal action — and those consequences attach to you and to the property, not to us.

Please gather and send your HOA documents now, then complete the short acknowledgment at the bottom of this page. We cannot begin marketing until we have it.

Why this matters

Our shared goal is to market your investment promptly and keep vacancy as short as possible. Everything we do is built around that. This is the one place where moving fast without the right information costs far more than it saves.

Every homeowners association, condominium association and master association has its own leasing requirements. Those commonly include an application the resident must submit, an approval period that can run weeks, application and transfer fees, caps or waiting periods on the number of leased units, minimum lease terms, rules governing pets, vehicles and occupancy, and — critically — a prohibition on any resident occupying the property before written association approval.

As the property owner and a member of the association, you and the property itself are legally subject to those rules. The association may impose fines, restrict occupancy, place a lien against the property, or pursue legal action for noncompliance. Because those consequences ultimately attach to you as the owner and to your property, obtaining and providing complete, current HOA information is your responsibility.

Why we can't simply get it for you

We will always make reasonable efforts to help, and often we succeed. But associations frequently do not respond to property managers, because we are neither the owner nor a member and they have no obligation to us. You are the member. A request from you carries weight that the same request from us does not.

Our assistance does not transfer your responsibility to Verandah. When we help, we are helping — not assuming the obligation.

What we need

Send us everything you have. If you are missing something, tell us what is missing rather than leaving a gap we don't know about.

Leasing rules and restrictions

Any cap on leased units, waiting period after purchase, minimum lease term, or restriction on leasing at all.

Tenant application requirements

The application form itself, where it is submitted, what the association requires with it, and how long approval takes.

All fees

Application fees, transfer fees, capital contributions, amenity or key-fob fees, vehicle decals — and who pays each one.

Occupancy rules

Whether written approval is required before move-in, occupancy limits, and any background or screening the association performs itself.

Pet, vehicle and parking rules

Breed, weight and number limits, registration requirements, guest parking, commercial vehicle restrictions.

Association contact information

The management company name, your account or unit number, and a direct contact.

Governing documents

Declaration, bylaws, rules and regulations, and any recent amendments — the current versions, not the ones from closing.

What happens when this information is missing

This is not hypothetical. Here is the sequence, and it is the reason this page exists:

We market the property and place a well-qualified resident.

Everything appears to go well. The lease is signed, the resident moves in, rent begins.

The association discovers the tenancy.

They inform us that an association application was required and was never submitted, and that the resident is occupying the property in violation of the governing documents.

The resident is now required to do something they never agreed to.

They signed a lease that said nothing about an association application, approval or fee. Some cooperate. Some refuse outright, and there is very little leverage to compel them.

Fines, legal action, and a compromised tenancy.

The association begins assessing fines against the property and may pursue legal action or a lien. The lease itself may be jeopardized. The costs and the exposure land on the owner.

Every step of that sequence traces back to one missing document at the beginning. It is entirely preventable, and the prevention takes an afternoon.

If your HOA information is incomplete

We understand the pressure to get to market. Vacancy is expensive and waiting on an association can be maddening. You may direct us to proceed while HOA information is still incomplete or unconfirmed, and the acknowledgment below gives you that option.

We want to be straightforward with you about what that authorization does and does not do. It documents that the decision was informed and that you directed it. It cannot override your association's rules, and it does not eliminate the association's ability to act against you or the property. No document we sign between us binds the association.

What we are relying on

We have no independent access to your association's requirements and no way to confirm that a requirement does not exist. When you tell us your association requires no approval before occupancy — or you tell us what the process is — we proceed on your word.

Your authorization allows us to market the property, execute a lease and permit your resident to move in, based on what you have told us. If an association requirement later comes to light that has not been met, we will tell you immediately and help you work through it. We may pause marketing, leasing or move-in while that happens. The requirement and its consequences remain yours.

This is why the questions on the form matter more than the documents. If you are not certain whether your association requires its own application and approval before a resident moves in, say you are not certain. "I don't know" is a useful answer and it costs you nothing. A confident "no" that turns out to be wrong is what creates the problem we are trying to avoid.

Complete your acknowledgment

Two or three minutes. You will confirm what you've provided, choose whether to proceed under the conditions above, and sign electronically. Marketing begins once this is submitted and your documents are received.
